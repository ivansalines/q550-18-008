# v18.50j — Early Cancellation Derivative Balance & Response-Flow Audit

## Purpose and scientific basis

The observed peak in Q550 v18.50i was a maximum of a **normalized sensitivity cancellation**, not a maximum of material stress. We compare the formal idea of an observed peak generated by competing rates with the independently developed glass constitutive framework of Singh, Ginzburg and Zaccone (*Microscopic constitutive theory of stress overshoot, yielding, and strain hardening in amorphous materials*, arXiv:2607.13734, 15 July 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13734). That paper concerns nonaffine elasticity, structural relaxation and viscous stress, with polymer strain hardening. We neither import its parameters nor claim an equivalence with Q550.

## Parent data and scope

The complete ZIP of Q550 v18.50i (with its earlier executable genealogy) is included and authenticated with CRC and SHA-256 (18/18 entries). The parent contains 4,890 vector samples from the original 11D/10D solver. This **v18.50j is an independent postprocessing audit**, with 30 curves (2 κ₀ values × 5 charts, including one redundant uniform-rescaling control × 3 kick amplitudes), 161 samples per curve, and 4,800 positive-horizon derivative ledger rows. It performs no new physical integration. Phase/horizon are represented by the original model coordinate `Δξ`, not physical strain, experimental time or a universal clock.

## Exact identity and rate decomposition

In any fixed declared four-dimensional sensitivity chart, define

\[
 b=\frac{\|T\|}{\|L\|},\qquad c=\frac{L\cdot T}{\|L\|\|T\|},\qquad
 C=1-\frac{\sqrt{1+b^2+2bc}}{1+b}.
\]

Let \(S=\sqrt{1+b^2+2bc}\). The exact chain-rule split, wherever these expressions are defined, is

\[
 C' = \left(\frac{S}{(1+b)^2}-\frac{b+c}{S(1+b)}\right)b'
       -\frac{b}{S(1+b)}c'
     =C'_{\mathrm{balance}}+C'_{\mathrm{orientation}}.
\]

This identity is algebraic. The two derivative terms measure changes in norm balance and relative orientation; they are not separately measured physical stresses. The derivatives are taken from cubic splines of the **original sampled** `b(Δξ)` and `c(Δξ)`.

## Central result: event ordering and cancellation

Reference: original κ₀ = 1/6, cubic r = 0.24, ε = 1e-4, common parent chart.

| Event | Numerical Δξ |
|---|---:|
| Norm equality `b=1` (spline estimate) | 0.0010591159234 |
| Stationarity `C'=0` (spline-chain-rule estimate) | 0.0010688353244 |
| Largest actual sampled cancellation | 0.0010750000 |

At interpolated stationarity, `C'_balance ≈ -4.098974709`, `C'_orientation ≈ +4.098974709` per model Δξ. Their sum is numerically zero by construction of the interpolated root. The **resultant norm** still increases at `d||R||/dΔξ ≈ +131.7700`, hence this is *not* a stationary point of `||R||`.

For autonomous κ₀ = 7π/132, corresponding estimates are equality at `0.0010587068195` and stationarity at `0.0010684237536`, with contributions about `−4.100081965` and `+4.100081965`, and resultant norm growth `+131.8643`.

The **displacement of the cancellation peak beyond norm equality** is explained by a continued change in relative orientation, quantified by `c'<0` in both parent charts in the zoom. No independent mechanical mechanism is inferred from this decomposition alone.

## What the Zaccone comparison does and does not establish

A material constitutive model may have a stress maximum where `dσ_total/dε=0` with `σ_total = σ_el + σ_visc + σ_hard`. In Q550, the cancellation scalar has a maximum where `dC/dΔξ≈0` but the **norm of the actual sensitivity response is increasing**. These are not the same physical or mathematical peak. What is comparable is the methodological need to inspect the rates of the component observables under a specified deformation/evolution coordinate.

The v18.50i input has no calibrated mapping between Q550 `L,T` and material elastic/viscous/hardening stresses, no constitutive strain variable, and no measured materials data. No such mapping is asserted here.

## Numerical limitations and next experiment

The spline derivative estimates are well-defined numerical estimates between sampled points but **not validated interval enclosures** or independently refined physical integrations. Differences in spline representation can shift interpolated roots inside the local sample bracket. The parent uses a nominal ε range 1e-5..1e-3. Event stability across these ε values is only local finite-amplitude evidence. An actual refinement should rerun the v18.50i solver on an adaptively dense event-centered grid for both κ₀ families and record the same derivatives from the variational equations where available.
